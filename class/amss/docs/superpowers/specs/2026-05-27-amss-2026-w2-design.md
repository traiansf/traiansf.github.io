# AMSS 2026 — Week 2 Lecture Design

**Status:** Approved design (2026-05-27). Ready for implementation planning.
**Owner:** Traian-Florin Șerbănuță
**Parent spec:** `2026-05-01-amss-ai-redesign-design.md` (course-level redesign).
**Sibling spec:** `2026-05-07-amss-2026-w1-design.md` (W1; established the per-week template, demo runbook contract, slide-density convention, verification-gate pattern).
**Decomposition strategy:** Sequential by week. This spec covers Week 2 only; subsequent weeks each get their own spec → plan → implement cycle.

## Goal

Author the W2 lecture deck (`class/amss-2026/curs/02-requirements.md`) plus its sibling demo runbook and fallback assets, replacing the current single-stub-slide placeholder. The deck delivers the four content threads the parent spec §2 mandates for W2 (functional / non-functional / domain requirements; prompting for elicitation; AI failure modes; use cases as a structuring lens) wrapped in one full architect-critic cycle on a bike-sharing requirements doc.

W2 is also the lecture half of the Lab 1 week; the lecture's demo is the forerunner of Lab 1's hands-on drill, and the lecture explicitly previews what Lab 1 asks students to do.

## Decisions (locked during brainstorm)

These apply to W2 specifically; the "course-wide" tag marks decisions that carry forward to W3-W14 and were originally locked in W1's spec.

- **Spine: mirror W1** — welcome → frame → demo → deeper-dive → close. The per-week template established in W1 §2 is reused unchanged for W2.
- **Demo scenario: city bike-sharing app.** Users rent and return bicycles at stations across a city; payment by app; staff rebalance bikes between stations. Mid-size, NFR-rich, naturally invites fabrication / over-spec / vague-NFR defects. Not used in Labs 3-4 (their reserved scenarios are parking lot, ATM, drone CAS per the 2025 lab catalogue).
- **W2 demo shape: single architect-critic cycle, use-case-scaffolded re-prompt.** Prompt #1 elicits a flat requirements list (AI fabricates, over-specs, writes vague NFRs); critique walks the failure-mode catalogue; prompt #2 re-imposes a use-case scaffold to demonstrate vocabulary tightening AI output. Same beat structure as W1's library-kiosk cycle.
- **Use-case depth: vocabulary only, no UML notation in W2.** "Use case = one user goal" is treated as a structural noun. UML notation (actors, ovals, system boundary, include/extend) lands in W6.
- **Time budget: ~81 min content + ~10-19 min slack.** Total content time nearly identical to W1 (W1's ~82 + 15-18 was deliberately under-filled to absorb logistics); W2 reallocates the saved welcome/frame minutes to four content threads instead. "Fuller" in §2's pacing-peg sense (content-weighted vs framing-weighted), not in total minutes. Q&A folds into each segment; no buffered Q&A block at the end.
- **Slide density: bullet-light, ≤5 bullets, ≤1 line/bullet.** *Course-wide.* Inherited from W1 §2.
- **F1+F3+F4 mentions: two — frame + close.** W1's spec made triple-mention a spine feature because W1 was the literacy-floor announcement. W2's spine is content; two anchored mentions (1st in frame, 2nd in close anchoring F4 traceability) suffice.
- **Demo runbook contract: same 10-section structure as W1's runbook.** *Course-wide pattern locked here* (setup, prompt #1 verbatim, defect catalogue of 6, critique walkthrough, prompt #2, recap, fallback, make-it-fail reserve, fallback assets, time budget, segment synergy).
- **Verification: solo dry-run is always sufficient.** *Course-wide.* Inherited from W1.
- **Source-material reuse from AMSS 2025.** `class/amss/curs/03-requirements.md`'s requirements-types content and the Denver Airport vignette survive into W2. The use-case + sequence-diagram halves of that file shift to W6.

## 2. Lecture spine, time budget, slide pacing

100-minute slot, ~81 minutes of planned content, ~10-19 minutes of natural slack. Q&A absorbed into each segment.

| # | Segment | Time | Slides | Pacing peg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome / agenda | 3 min | 1 | Single slide; very brief — no re-introducing |
| 2 | **Frame: connect to W1 → today's spine** | 8 min | 3-4 | 2 min/slide; refresh architect-critic, name the 4 threads, F1+F3+F4 anchor (1st of 2 mentions) |
| 3 | **Demo: bike-sharing requirements, 1 cycle** | 15 min | 1 trigger + runbook | Slide stays up the whole demo |
| 4 | Requirements types (FR / NFR / domain) | 10 min | 4-5 | 2 min/slide; reuse 2025 Denver Airport vignette |
| 5 | Prompting for elicitation | 10 min | 4-5 | 2 min/slide; direct vs scaffolded prompting |
| 6 | **AI failure modes catalogue** | 15 min | 5-7 | 2-3 min/slide; the central content payload |
| 7 | Use cases as structuring lens | 10 min | 3-4 | 2-3 min/slide; vocabulary only, no notation |
| 8 | Bridge to W3 + Lab 1 preview + close | 10 min | 3-4 | 2-3 min/slide; F4 (2nd mention), Lab 1 deliverable, W3 teaser |
| **Total** | | **~81 min content** | **~25-30 slides** | + ~10-19 min slack |

**Slide-density convention.** Same as W1: bullet-light, no slide carries more than ~5 bullets, no bullet runs more than one line. Where a topic needs more text, it goes into the `::: notes` speaker block.

**Slide counts are budgets, not floors.** §5 below specifies *required* slides per segment. The §2 ranges include 1-2 slots per segment for elaboration the speaker chooses during authoring — an intro slide, a recap slide, an extra example. Required content covers roughly 70-80% of each segment's count; the remainder is judgment.

**Variance from W1.** W2 averages ~3 min/slide vs W1's ~2 min/slide — content carries definitions and worked examples that need read time. Slide count is consequently lower (25-30 vs W1's 30-35) even though total content time is higher. Acceptable.

## 4. Demo runbook contract

`curs/02-requirements-demo.md` contains the same 10 fixed sections as W1's runbook — same structure, W2-specific content.

### 4.1 Setup (pre-class checklist, ~1 min)

Continue.dev open against the canonical course endpoint, agentic chat mode. A markdown preview pane open (the demo's artifact is text, not a diagram — students need to read the requirements doc as it appears). Fallback PNGs queued in a tab. Demo trigger slide on screen.

### 4.2 Architect prompt #1 (verbatim)

> *"Generate a requirements document for a city bike-sharing app: users rent and return bicycles at stations across a city; payment is by app; staff rebalance bikes between stations. Cover functional, non-functional, and domain requirements."*

Pasted as-is — no live improvisation. Reproducibility outweighs naturalness.

### 4.3 Defect catalogue (menu of 6, pick 2-3 live)

Each entry: defect description, what to point at, critique question for the room. Over-spec'ing the catalogue is intentional — robustness against model variance.

| # | Defect | Where to point | Critique question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fabricated stakeholder** | "City Hall API integration" or similar role with no link to user need | *"Where did this stakeholder come from in my prompt? Walk it back."* |
| 2 | **Vague NFR** | "The system shall be performant / scalable / user-friendly" | *"Can you write a test that proves this? If not, it's not a requirement."* |
| 3 | **Over-specification** | 30+ requirements when 8 would do — separate REQs for theft prevention, GPS jamming, weather sensors | *"Read the top 5 — does the system fail without each one? What's actually load-bearing?"* |
| 4 | **Fabricated technology** | "REQ: the system shall use Redis for caching" / "PostgreSQL backend" | *"Why is a database choice in the requirements? What user need does Redis serve?"* |
| 5 | **Conflated requirements** | One FR bundles distinct concerns ("user can rent, return, and report stolen bikes") | *"Split this — what's the success criterion for each piece?"* |
| 6 | **Missing acceptance criterion** | "Users should be able to rent a bike" with no measurable success | *"How do I know when this is done?"* |

Speaker picks the 2-3 that actually appear in live AI output.

### 4.4 Critique walkthrough (~5 min)

Speaker walks the chosen 2-3 defects. Pedagogical move named explicitly the first time:

> *"That's the **critic** half — reading what AI produced and naming what's missing or wrong. Next I'm doing the **architect** half — I'll re-prompt with structure."*

### 4.5 Architect prompt #2 (constructed live, use-case scaffold)

Template provided in the runbook:

> *"Rewrite the requirements organized by use case. Each use case is one user goal. For each: who does it, what's the success criterion, and one realistic non-functional constraint. Drop fabricated stakeholders. Drop technology choices — those are implementation, not requirements."*

The use-case-scaffold instruction is the pedagogical pivot — it shows how vocabulary changes AI output quality. ~1 min to type, ~2-3 min for AI to revise.

### 4.6 Recap (~1 min)

> *"One architect-critic cycle on a requirements doc. The first pass produced everything plausible-sounding; the critic half found the failure-mode patterns; the architect half re-prompted with a use-case scaffold — and the second pass tightened automatically. That scaffold pattern is what Lab 1 asks you to do hands-on this week."*

Points back at "two roles" slide from frame.

### 4.7 Make-it-fail reserve

If AI's first output is suspiciously clean (low probability for a long-form requirements doc, but possible):

> *"Now redo this assuming the city has 50,000 daily riders and 2,000 bikes across 200 stations."*

This near-certainly triggers fabrication (peak-hour rebalancing routes, predictive ML for demand forecasting, etc.), restoring the critique surface.

### 4.8 Fallback assets

`02-requirements-demo-fallback/` contains three committed PNGs, captured during the solo dry-run:

| Filename | Contents |
|---|---|
| `01-fallback-cycle1-output.png` | Seed AI requirements doc with ≥2 visible defects |
| `02-fallback-cycle1-revised.png` | Post-critique re-prompted output |
| `03-fallback-make-it-fail.png` | The make-it-fail reserve's output |

When the procurement decision changes the canonical endpoint, the dry-run reruns and the PNGs refresh.

### 4.9 Time budget reconciliation

Prompt #1 typed (~1 min) + AI generates (~2-3 min, longer than W1 because the artifact is text) + critique walkthrough (~5 min) + prompt #2 typed (~1 min) + AI revises (~2-3 min) + recap (~1 min) = 12-14 min. Variance lives in AI generation latency; runbook does not pad those moments — speaker uses them to predict aloud what they expect.

### 4.10 Lab 1 synergy

The demo is the forerunner of Lab 1's drill. Students replicate the same loop on their own toy domain in pairs. The runbook's prompt #2 scaffold (use-case + measurable + drop-technology) is the rubric Lab 1's reflection asks them to apply against their own AI output.

## 5. Content contract per segment

For each segment, the **goal** (what students walk out understanding) and **required content** (must-include slides). Slide-by-slide bullets are an implementation concern, not a spec concern.

### 5.1 Welcome / agenda (3 min)

**Goal:** students know what today covers and how it connects to W1 and Lab 1.

**Required content:** one agenda slide listing the spine + the one-line "today is content; Lab 1 this week is your hands-on follow-through".

### 5.2 Frame — connect to W1, name today's spine (8 min)

**Goal:** students recognize today as one instance of the architect-critic loop applied to requirements, and recognize F1+F3+F4 as the rubric for the rest of the course.

**Required content:**
- "Recap: architect + critic" slide — both roles named again (1 line each); cite W1.
- "Today's loop runs on requirements" — name the artifact this week's loop produces.
- "Four threads in today's lecture" — preview FR/NFR/domain, prompting, failure modes, use-case scaffold.
- F1+F3+F4 anchor slide (1st of 2 mentions) — point at F1 (critique today's output) and F4 (traceability lives at requirement → use case → ... → test).

### 5.3 Demo trigger (15 min)

**Goal:** students see one full architect-critic cycle on a requirements doc and observe how a use-case scaffold tightens AI output.

**Required content:** single slide with the bike-sharing prompt visible, runbook reference, "watch for fabrication, vague NFR, and over-spec'ing" reminder. Speaker drives the runbook (§4) for 12-14 min.

### 5.4 Requirements types (10 min)

**Goal:** students can name functional / non-functional / domain requirements and pick the right type for a given statement.

**Required content:**
- Definitions slide — three types side-by-side with one-line examples.
- One slide each showing AI failing differently for each type (foreshadow §5.6's failure-mode catalogue).
- Denver Airport vignette (1 slide) — the cost of unclear/changing requirements. Reused from 2025's `03-requirements.md` (verification gate §6 documents this reuse).

### 5.5 Prompting for elicitation (10 min)

**Goal:** students know that *how* you prompt determines what AI elicits, and can name two prompting moves.

**Required content:**
- "Direct vs scaffolded prompting" — side-by-side example.
- Stakeholder-role priming — *"Act as a domain expert in X; produce requirements from that role."*
- Negative prompting — explicitly say what NOT to include (technology choices, vague adjectives, fabricated roles).
- One slide naming Lab 1's prompting drill explicitly.

### 5.6 AI failure modes catalogue (15 min) — central content payload

**Goal:** students can name the three patterns (fabrication, over-specification, vague NFRs) and recognize them in unseen AI output (this is F1 in practice).

**Required content:**
- One slide per failure mode (three core slides), with: name, definition (1 line), realistic example, critique question that surfaces it.
- Two further slides extending the catalogue with named secondary failures: conflated requirements, fabricated technology choices.
- One transition slide tying these failure modes to the demo students just watched ("you saw mode X live in cycle 1").
- Optional reflection slide — "what defect did *you* spot first?" — fits the segment's slide budget if used.

### 5.7 Use cases as structuring lens (10 min) — vocabulary only, no notation

**Goal:** students understand "use case = one user goal" and know that organizing requirements by use case is the simplest scaffold for tightening AI output.

**Required content:**
- "Use case = one user goal" definition slide.
- One slide showing the demo's use-case-scaffolded re-prompt as the canonical example of vocabulary tightening output.
- One slide explicitly handing off to W6: *"UML notation for use cases (actors, ovals, include/extend) lands in W6. Today we use them as a structural noun, not a diagram."*

**No UML use-case notation appears in W2 slides outside the explicit W6-handoff slide.**

### 5.8 Bridge to W3 + Lab 1 preview + close (10 min)

**Goal:** students know what Lab 1 asks of them this week, how W3 reframes the same loop as "testable specs," and where traceability (F4) starts.

**Required content:**
- Lab 1 preview slide — domain assignment mechanism, time budget, deliverable (requirements doc + 5-line reflection on AI failure modes), where it gets committed. Vague enough to survive Lab 1's spec still being open (§8).
- W3 teaser — *"if AI can't produce a passing test from your spec, your spec is too vague"* (the W3 mantra; foreshadows the testable-specs loop).
- F1+F3+F4 anchor slide (2nd of 2 mentions) — explicitly point at F4 (traceability starts at requirements; if requirements are fabricated, every downstream artifact inherits the fabrication).
- Closer slide — "next week: testable specs; this week's lab: do this loop yourselves on a toy domain".

### 5.9 Deliberate omissions

Not in W2, by design:

- **UML use-case notation.** W6 owns this. Use-case content in W2 stays at vocabulary level.
- **Sequence diagrams.** 2025 lecture 03 covered these; pushed to W6.
- **Specific elicitation methods** (interviews, observation, ethnography) — out of scope for an AI-mediated course. The "elicitation" we teach is prompting AI.
- **Requirements-document standards / templates** (IEEE 830, user-story templates, MoSCoW) — name-drop optional, no slide.
- **TDD / testable specs.** W3's job. W2's bridge slide is a single teaser sentence.
- **Bullet-by-bullet slide authorship.** Implementation, not spec.

## 6. Verification

The lecture is "done" when all of:

1. **Pandoc build succeeds.** `make -C class/amss-2026/curs class/amss2026/curs/02-requirements.html` and `02-requirements.pdf` both produce well-formed output. No build warnings about missing `::: notes` blocks.
2. **Slide count and pacing match the budget.** Manual count totals ~25-32 slides; per-segment subsets fall within §2's ranges.
3. **F1+F3+F4 mentioned exactly twice** (frame, close). Triple-mention is W1's spine, not W2's. Drift either direction is a spec deviation.
4. **Demo runbook is dry-run reproducible.** Instructor runs `02-requirements-demo.md` end-to-end against the chosen course endpoint at least once before delivery; captures live AI output as seed for the fallback PNGs; confirms the defect catalogue covers what the model actually produced. *This step blocks delivery.*
5. **Fallback PNGs exist** in `02-requirements-demo-fallback/` per §4.8 (3 files, named as specified).
6. **`static/index.html` links resolve.** `class/amss2026/index.html` → `curs/02-requirements.html` and `curs/02-requirements.pdf` both load.
7. **Cross-reference linting.** Every reference to another week (W1 recap, W3 teaser, W6 use-case-notation handoff, W10 traceability) or to Lab 1 names a session that exists in parent-spec §2/§3 tables.
8. **No UML use-case notation in deck slide bodies.** `grep -E "actor|<<include>>|<<extend>>|oval" 02-requirements.md` produces no hits in slide bodies (occurrences allowed only on the §5.7 W6-handoff slide, which explicitly names what's deferred).
9. **`*-demo.md` filter still in effect.** Confirm `02-requirements-demo.md` is not picked up by the curs build (the W1 Makefile filter covers it by name pattern; verify it actually does).
10. **Source-material reuse documented.** Where W2 reuses content from `class/amss/curs/03-requirements.md` (requirements-types definitions, Denver Airport vignette), the deck's `::: notes` cite the 2025 source so future editors know what was carried forward vs. authored fresh.

**Implicit dependencies:**

- Step 4 needs a working course endpoint. If procurement (parent spec §6) is still open at dry-run time, dry-run uses Gemini free-tier (option 2) and the fallback PNGs are captured from that. Lecture content does not change based on which endpoint is chosen.
- No automated test for slide content correctness. Editorial check ("does this teach what it should teach?") is judgment, not lint.
