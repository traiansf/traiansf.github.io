## Goal

Author the Lab 2 student brief (`class/amss-2026/lab/Lab02.md`) plus an instructor runbook (`class/amss-2026/lab/Lab02-instructor.md`), replacing the current single-stub placeholder. Lab 2 is the second skill-drill lab and the hands-on follow-through of the W4 lecture (class diagrams in the AI loop): **individually**, each student drives AI to produce a UML class diagram from a provided 1-page spec, runs W4's 4-step read-order against the output, iterates **at least twice**, and commits the best PlantUML diagram plus a critique log (≤1 page).

## Decisions (locked during brainstorm)

The "course-wide" tag marks decisions that reuse or extend a Lab 1 course-wide baseline.

- **Toy domain: single shared domain — library kiosk.** Every student works the same domain. Universally familiar (everyone has borrowed a book), compact enough for a 70-min solo drill, and structurally rich: it forces every element of the W4 reading floor. Specifically it carries the **Book-vs-Copy identity distinction** (a *title* vs a *physical item*) that AI reliably collapses, plus whole-part ownership (a library *holds* copies), one-to-one/​one-to-many loan multiplicities, and member subtypes (is-a). Not bike-sharing (the W4 demo domain), not vending machine (Lab 1), and not parking lot / ATM / drone CAS (reserved for the Lab 3–4 critique labs per parent spec §3 and the 2025 lab catalogue). Library kiosk is the parent spec's own named example for a toy structural domain (§6 cross-lab principles).
- **Honest spec with natural traps — not a planted-contradiction spec.** The 1-page input spec states the domain truthfully and contains **no deliberate lies or self-contradictions**. The defects students catch emerge from *AI's own tendencies* read against an honest spec — exactly the Lab 1 philosophy (a clean vending-machine domain whose bare prompt reliably produced fabrication and omission). The spec's wording deliberately *surfaces* the facts AI most often flattens (Book vs Copy; the library holds copies that outlive any loan; a loan is exactly one copy to one member; members come in kinds with different loan limits), so the W4 defect catalogue fires naturally. Hunting *planted contradictions* is the Lab 3 red-team job; Lab 2 drills driving + reading, not contradiction-spotting.
- **Individual, not paired.** Parent spec §3 row 2 specifies *individual* work ("70 min individual"). This is the deliberate contrast with Lab 1's pair drill: Lab 2 makes each student personally drive *and* critique, because the oral defense (the integrity check) is individual and cold. No architect/critic role-swap — each student plays both halves of the loop alone, mirroring the solo F1 drill from the W4 deck.
- **Two mandated iterations.** Parent spec and the W4 preview slide both say "iterate ≥2 times." Concretely: a bare Round-1 prompt, then **two required re-prompts** (re-prompt #1 fixes the highest-severity defects via a domain-constraint scaffold; re-prompt #2 addresses residual defects — typically the god class / invented infrastructure / is-a-vs-attribute). The student keeps the *best* diagram, not the first. (Contrast Lab 1's "iterate at least once.")
- **Rubric = the W4 read-order, verbatim.** The critique log is structured by the 4-step read-order taught in `04-class-diagrams.md` ("How to Read an AI Class Diagram"): (1) are the classes real domain concepts? (2) are the multiplicities right (read each aloud)? (3) does each association name a real relationship? (4) are whole-part relationships captured? Defects are named from W4's 5-entry catalogue. This lab does not invent new vocabulary — it applies what W4 taught.
- **Submission: branch push per student.** Each student pushes to `lab02/<student-id>` in the course lab repo. Same lowest-friction baseline as Lab 1, scoped to individuals (`s01`…`sNN`) rather than pairs. No PR for Lab 2 (PR-based review enters in the later critique labs). *Course-wide* baseline carried from Lab 1.
- **Grading: completion + critique-log gate (pass/redo, low-stakes).** Pass requires both deliverable files committed AND a critique log that correctly names ≥2 distinct W4 defects with a real re-prompt rationale (F3) for at least one iteration. A vacuous log is a redo, not a fail. Same low-stakes literacy-floor bar as Lab 1 — the lab assesses *what the student did to the diagram*, never the diagram's polish. *Course-wide* grading philosophy from Lab 1.
- **The 1-page spec is the input artifact, seeded into the lab repo.** Where Lab 1 seeded a `lab01/README.md` brief, Lab 2 seeds a `lab02/README.md` that carries the brief **and** the honest 1-page library-kiosk spec students feed to AI. The spec text is authored here (paste-ready in the instructor runbook), not improvised at lab time.
- **Two source artifacts, student/instructor split.** A published student brief plus an unpublished instructor runbook — the *course-wide* lab pattern established in Lab 1 via the `%-instructor.md` Makefile filter (already present; §1). No build-config change needed.

## 2. Session spine, time budget

100-minute lab slot. The 10 / 70 / 20 split is locked by parent spec §3 row 2 and echoed on W4's preview slide.

| # | Phase | Time | Owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brief + spec read | 10 min | instructor-led | hand out the 1-page spec; restate the read-order rubric and the ≥2-iteration mandate (§4) |
| 2 | Individual drive-and-critique drill | 70 min | each student | Round 1 + two required re-prompts; library kiosk (§5) |

The 70-min drill is the only compressible phase. Brief overruns eat into drill setup, not the share-out — the share-out is the pedagogical payoff and is protected. §10 specifies how the drill degrades gracefully under time pressure (the second re-prompt is the buffer).

This week's lab has **no in-lab tooling onboarding** — students activated their endpoint in Lab 1 (W2) and arrive with a working Continue.dev. The 10-min brief is content, not setup. Students who join late or whose endpoint regressed fall back per §10.

## 3. W4 lecture synergy — the locked contract this lab fulfills

W4 (the lecture half of this week) previewed Lab 2 on three slides. Their wording is the contract this spec must honor without material change:

- **Slide "Apply It to Your Own Output"** (`04-class-diagrams.md`): *"This week's lab (Lab 2): you drive AI to a class diagram from a 1-page spec, then run *this* read-order against what it draws. The critique log is the evidence — what you caught and how you fixed it."*
- **Slide "This Week's Lab: Lab 2":** *"Drive AI to produce a class diagram from a 1-page spec. Iterate at least twice; log what changed and why. Deliverable: the PlantUML diagram + a critique log (1 page max). Commit to the course lab repo."*
- **Slide "That's It For Today":** *"This week's lab (Lab 2): drive AI to a class diagram, then critique it."*

What this lab inherits from W4 and must reuse, not reinvent:

- **The 4-step read-order** (`04-class-diagrams.md`, slide "How to Read an AI Class Diagram"): (1) real domain classes? (2) multiplicities right (read each aloud)? (3) each association a real relationship? (4) whole-part captured? This *is* the critique-log rubric.
- **The five named defects** students learned in W4's catalogue: **wrong multiplicity**, **fake / decorative association**, **missing aggregation** (whole-part flattened to a plain line), **invented class / infrastructure** (`DatabaseManager`, `CacheController` — implementation, not domain), and **god class** (one class that does everything). These are the vocabulary the critique log must use.
- **The reading floor** (W4, slide "Beyond the Floor"): class + three compartments, association + multiplicity, aggregation/composition, generalization. Interfaces / abstract classes / dependency arrows are explicitly *off* the floor — the brief may note that AI emits them but does not require students to critique them.
- **The critique loop on structure** (W4, slide "The Critique Loop on Structure"): read → name the defects → re-prompt with domain constraints → re-read. Students replicate this solo on the library kiosk; the re-prompt scaffold is "name the domain rules" (e.g., *a rental is one bike* → here, *a loan is exactly one copy*), exactly the W4 demo's prompt #2 move.

If any mechanic below would alter the deliverable as students heard it in W4, that is a spec error — flag it, do not silently diverge. (If a *future* revision genuinely needs to change the deliverable, W4's three preview slides need a synced one-line update.)

## 4. Brief + spec read (10 min)

Instructor-led. Students arrive with a working Continue.dev (from Lab 1); this phase is content, not setup.

1. **0–4 min — hand out + read the 1-page spec.** Distribute the library-kiosk spec (it is also in `lab02/README.md` on clone). Students read it once. The instructor calls out that it is an *honest* spec — no planted tricks — and that the defects will come from the AI, not the spec.
2. **4–8 min — restate the rubric.** Put the W4 4-step read-order and the 5-defect catalogue back on screen. Restate the mandate: bare prompt → critique → **two** required re-prompts → keep the best diagram. The critique log is structured by the read-order.
3. **8–10 min — confirm access.** Each student confirms they can reach the lab repo and create branch `lab02/<student-id>` (most still have the clone from Lab 1). Instructor confirms endpoints are live; anyone whose endpoint regressed switches to the Gemini fallback now (§10).

Exit criterion: every student has read the spec, has the rubric in front of them, and can push to `lab02/<student-id>`.

## 5. The individual drill (70 min)

Domain: **library kiosk.** Single shared domain for all students. Each student plays both halves of the architect-critic loop alone.

### 5.1 Setup (0–5 min)
Student opens a working `lab02/<student-id>/` directory with a scratch `critique-log.md` (the running log is the F1/F3 evidence and becomes the deliverable). Diagram source goes in `diagram.puml`.

### 5.2 Round 1 — bare prompt + first read (5–25 min, ~20 min)
- Student sends a deliberately under-scaffolded seed prompt (mirrors the W4 demo prompt #1), pasting the 1-page spec:
  > *"Generate a UML class diagram (as PlantUML) for this library system. [paste the 1-page spec]"*
- AI produces a draft. Student renders it (PlantUML preview) and runs the **4-step read-order**, logging each defect by W4 name + a severity (high/med/low) + where it appears. The library-kiosk defect card (§5.5) is the safety net if the draft looks suspiciously clean.

### 5.3 Re-prompt #1 — domain-constraint scaffold (25–45 min, ~20 min)
- Student re-prompts to fix the highest-severity defects, naming the domain rules the AI got wrong — e.g., *"A Book is a title; a Copy is a physical item — model both. A loan is exactly one copy to one member. The library holds many copies that outlive any loan (aggregation)."*
- AI regenerates; student re-reads with the same 4-step order, logs what changed. **This is mandated iteration #1.**

### 5.4 Re-prompt #2 — residual defects (45–62 min, ~17 min)
- Student targets the residual catalogue entries the first re-prompt usually leaves: a god `LibrarySystem` class, invented infrastructure (`DatabaseManager`), or member kinds modelled as a string flag instead of generalization. Re-prompt with the specific fix (*"Drop any class that isn't a library-domain concept. Model member kinds as subclasses, not a type field."*).
- AI regenerates; student re-reads and logs. **This is mandated iteration #2.**

### 5.5 Finalize + commit (62–70 min)
- Keep the best `diagram.puml` (tightest iteration, not the first draft).
- Finish `critique-log.md` — ≤1 page, structured by the read-order (§6.2).
- Commit both to branch `lab02/<student-id>`; push.

### 5.6 Library-kiosk defect card (the critic's safety net)
Given to students (not secret). If an AI draft looks complete, the student stresses these reliably-occurring defects — each maps to one or more W4 catalogue entries:

| What to check | Likely defect (W4 name) |
|---|---|
| `Member "*" -- "*" Book` (or Loan to many copies) | wrong multiplicity (a loan is one copy to one member) |
| Book and Copy collapsed into one class | wrong multiplicity / missing class (title vs physical item) |
| `Library -- Copy` as a plain line | missing aggregation (the library *holds* copies) |
| A line between, e.g., `Member` and `Library` with no verb | fake / decorative association |
| `LibrarySystem` class with all the data + `doEverything()` | god class |
| `DatabaseManager`, `CacheController`, `NotificationService` | invented class / infrastructure (not domain) |
| Member kind as a `type: String` attribute | is-a modelled as attribute (should be generalization) |
| `Member.loans: String` instead of a link to `Loan` | attribute-that-should-be-an-association |

(Note: the central, most-instructive trap is **Book vs Copy** — AI overwhelmingly models a single `Book` class and then can't express "two members borrow two *copies* of the same title," producing a wrong many-to-many. Surfacing that is the lab's signature catch.)

## 6. Deliverable spec

### 6.1 Files (per student, on branch `lab02/<student-id>`)
- `lab02/<student-id>/diagram.puml` — the final AI-driven PlantUML class diagram (best iteration). Must render. No size mandate beyond covering the domain.
- `lab02/<student-id>/critique-log.md` — **≤1 page**, structured per §6.2. This is the graded literacy artifact.
- `lab02/<student-id>/transcript.md` — *optional but recommended:* the raw prompt/output trail. Not required by the gate, but it is the concrete F3 evidence and helps the instructor adjudicate borderline logs.

### 6.2 Critique-log structure (≤1 page)
The contract says "critique log — what you caught and how you fixed it." The log is structured by the W4 read-order, one short block per iteration:

**Per iteration (Round 1, re-prompt #1, re-prompt #2):**
- **Defects found**, each: W4 name (multiplicity / fake association / missing aggregation / invented class / god class), where, severity.
- **Re-prompt move + why** — which domain rule you named and the rationale. *(F3 — the graded line.)*
- **What changed** after regenerating. *(F1)*

**Closing line:** one **residual risk** — a defect the AI never got right even after two iterations. *(seeds F4: a wrong structure propagates to tests and code.)*

A passing log names defects from the W4 vocabulary and gives a real reason for at least one re-prompt — not "the diagram was wrong, I asked again."

### 6.3 Student-id scheme
Instructor-assigned, handed out at the brief (e.g., `s01`…`sNN`). Documented in the instructor runbook so branch names and lab-repo directories are predictable and collision-free.

## 8. Grading — completion + critique-log gate

Low-stakes, pass/redo. Applied by the instructor against each pushed branch.

**Pass requires both:**
1. **Completion:** `diagram.puml` (renders) and `critique-log.md` both committed to `lab02/<student-id>` by the deadline.
2. **Critique-log gate:** the log (a) correctly names ≥2 distinct W4 defects (from the five: wrong multiplicity, fake/decorative association, missing aggregation, invented class/infrastructure, god class), and (b) gives a genuine re-prompt rationale (F3) for at least one iteration — *why* that domain rule was named, not just *what* was typed.

**Redo (not fail):** vacuous log — no named defect, or no rationale (e.g., "the diagram was wrong, I fixed it"). The student revises and re-pushes. The point is to enforce the literacy floor, not to punish.

**Explicitly not graded:** diagram polish/completeness, AI output quality, number of iterations beyond the two mandated. Grading the diagram would reward the wrong skill; this lab assesses critique and rationale (F1/F3), not generation.

The instructor runbook (§11) gives a worked example of a passing vs. a redo critique log so grading is consistent and fast across the cohort.

## 9. Share-out (20 min)

The payoff of the single shared domain: same spec, ~100 students, comparable defect data on structure.

1. **0–3 min — frame.** "Same spec, same domain — let's see which defects the AI produced across the room."
2. **3–14 min — 3–4 students present (~3 min each).** Each gives the **defect they caught that mattered most** + the **re-prompt move that fixed it** (or didn't). Pre-selected by the instructor during the drill from a quick scan of pushed logs (aim for variety across the five defects), with one volunteer slot. The **Book-vs-Copy** catch is worth surfacing if any student found it.
3. **14–18 min — live defect-frequency tally.** Hands-up per defect: *"Whose AI wrote a wrong multiplicity? A fake association? Flattened a whole-part? Invented infrastructure? Built a god class?"* Instructor tallies on the board. This frequency data is only possible because everyone shared a spec.
4. **18–20 min — close.** Tie the tally to F1/F4: the defects you tallied are exactly what you critique every week and in the oral defense; a wrong class structure propagates to the sequence diagrams (W6), the tests, and the code. Bridge to W5 (other structural views) and Lab 3 (the critique/red-team lab where you hunt *planted* defects in instructor-prepared artifacts).

## 10. Fallback plans

| Risk | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Endpoint regressed since Lab 1 | Switch config to Gemini free-tier (SETUP.md); instructor keeps a few spare credentials. |
| PlantUML preview not rendering | Use any online PlantUML renderer, or read the `.puml` source directly — the critique is about the *model*, not the rendering. |
| `git push` fails (auth/network) | Submit by pasting both files into a shared doc / emailing the instructor; fix git after class. Deadline accepts the fallback-submission timestamp. |
| AI output unexpectedly clean (little to critique) | Student uses the §5.6 defect card to stress specific checks; a "too-clean" draft that silently collapses Book/Copy or flattens the holds-aggregation *is* the defect. |
| Drill running long | The second re-prompt is the buffer. Minimum viable = Round 1 + re-prompt #1 + a log naming ≥2 defects. But the gate wants two iterations — only relax to one under genuine time loss, and say so in the share-out. |
| Brief overruns past 10 min | Eats Round 1 setup, never the share-out. If badly delayed, protect the 20-min share-out and let students do one solid re-prompt instead of two. |
| Student didn't internalize W4 read-order | The brief restates all four steps and the five defect names on its own slides — self-contained, no need to re-watch the lecture. |
