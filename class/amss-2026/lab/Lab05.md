# Lab 5: Workshop + Checkpoint

Two phases, 100 minutes — **the labs now turn to your own project.**

1. **Team workshop** (~60 min) — supervised project work; close the gaps you know about.
2. **Checkpoint defense** (~40 min) — each student gives a **3-minute cold defense** of their slice.

This is the **December checkpoint** — a **1-point gate** toward your project grade.

::: notes
The first project lab. Labs 1-4 drilled skills and critique; from here the labs are your own project. Today is workshop time plus the intermediate checkpoint preserved from the course — a low-stakes but real 1-point gate, and a rehearsal for the W14 defense.
:::

---

# What the Checkpoint Is

- A **3-minute, cold defense** of your slice's progress — no slides required, no AI in the room.
- The instructor asks **F1 / F3 / F4** questions about *your* slice.
- Worth **1 point** of the project grade. The bar is *progress and ownership*, not a finished slice.

::: notes
Set expectations: this is mid-project, so the slice is not expected to be done. What's checked is that the student owns it — can read their diagrams, justify their direction, and trace their slice. The 1 point is a gate, not a fine-grained score.
:::

---

# The Three Questions (from W12)

The checkpoint is the W12 rubric in miniature:

- **F1** — "Read me this diagram from your slice. Anything wrong?"
- **F3** — "Why did you direct AI this way here? What did you reject?"
- **F4** — "Walk one requirement of your slice to a test."

Same rubric as the W14 defense — this is your first live rehearsal.

::: notes
The checkpoint uses the exact F1+F3+F4 rubric from W12, scaled to 3 minutes. Students who internalise it now defend cleanly in W14. Point them back to the W12 strong-vs-weak answer patterns.
:::

---

# What to Have Ready

By the checkpoint, your slice should show:

- A **requirement** you own, traceable into your slice.
- The **two diagrams** (structural + behavioral) in progress — AI-generated, **reviewed by you**.
- A **defect log** started — at least a few caught defects.
- A **trace** you can walk, even if partial.

::: notes
The minimum viable slice for the checkpoint. None need be complete, but each must exist and be the student's own. The defect log and the reviewed diagrams are the F1/F3 evidence; the partial trace is the F4 evidence.
:::

---

# Use the Workshop Time

The first 60 minutes are supervised work. Spend them on:

- Running your **trace audit** (W10) — fix broken links before the checkpoint.
- **Re-reading** every AI diagram in your slice — could you critique it cold?
- Logging any **defects** you haven't yet written down.

The instructor circulates — ask for help.

::: notes
The workshop is targeted prep, not open coding. Steer students toward the trace audit and re-reading their own artifacts — exactly what the checkpoint (and W14) tests. This is also where the instructor spots who is behind.
:::

---

# What "Pass" Looks Like

You pass the checkpoint if, in 3 minutes, you can:

- **read** one of your diagrams and spot or confirm an issue (F1),
- **say why** you directed AI a certain way (F3),
- **walk** one requirement-to-test trace of your slice (F4).

Owning your slice — not finishing it — is the bar.

::: notes
The pass criteria, stated plainly so students can self-assess. A student who can do all three on a partial slice passes; one who can't read their own diagram or say why they made a choice does not — and gets concrete feedback to fix before W14.
:::

---

# If Your Slice Is Thin

Better to defend a **small, owned** slice than a large one you can't explain.

- Bring the part you understand best.
- Be honest about what's not done — honesty is a strength signal (W12).

The checkpoint is a safety net: it surfaces gaps while there's still time.

::: notes
Reassure students who are behind. The checkpoint's value is diagnostic — surfacing gaps two weeks before W14. A candid "this part is thin because X, here's my plan" defends better than an overclaim. Mirrors the W12 honesty point.
:::

---

# Submit / Record

- No new artifacts to submit — your slice lives in the **team repo** already.
- The instructor records your checkpoint result (pass / partial) on the spot.
- Partial? You get specific gaps to close before **W14**.

::: notes
Logistics. Nothing to push beyond the ongoing repo work. The instructor records the gate result live and hands partial students an explicit gap list. Keep it lightweight — the point is the rehearsal and the diagnosis.
:::

---

# Why This Matters

The checkpoint is your **first cold defense** — the W14 oral defense in miniature, with a safety net.

A student who can walk their slice today walks the whole project in W14. Next: **Lab 6** is the full dry run.

::: notes
Closer. Tie the checkpoint to the W14 endgame: it is the same skill, rehearsed early. Point forward to Lab 6 (the full dry run) and the W14 final defense. The 1 point is small; the rehearsal value is large.
