# Lab 6: Workshop + Dry Run

Two phases, 100 minutes — **the full rehearsal for Week 14.**

1. **Team workshop** (~50 min) — supervised work; close the gaps from the Lab 5 checkpoint.
2. **Cold-defense dry run** (~50 min) — the instructor picks students **at random** and drills the three defense skills: Critique, Rationale, Traceability.

Deliverable: **dry-run feedback** — informal, but every gap surfaced must be fixed before Week 14.

::: notes
The second project lab and the dress rehearsal. Lab 5 was a 3-minute checkpoint; today is the full cold defense across Critique, Rationale, and Traceability, with random selection so you can't predict the question. Ungraded — but the gaps it surfaces are your Week 14 to-do list.
:::

---

# What the Dry Run Is

- A **full cold defense** — unaided, no AI, no slides.
- The instructor calls on students **at random** and asks Critique / Rationale / Traceability questions about their slice.
- **Ungraded** — but treated as the real thing. Gaps become your pre-Week 14 checklist.

::: notes
This is the Week 14 format, run as a rehearsal. Random selection mirrors the real defense — the skill must be general, not a memorised script. Stress that ungraded does not mean low-stakes: this is the last live feedback before the graded defense.
:::

---

# Random Selection — Be Ready Cold

You don't know **when** you'll be called or **which** question you'll get.

- The examiner may show **another team's** diagram for a Critique question.
- You can't rehearse a script — you rehearse the *skills*.

That's the point: Week 14 is the same.

::: notes
Random, cold selection is the core mechanic. It defeats memorisation and forces the general skill — exactly what Week 14 tests. Showing another team's diagram for a Critique question reinforces that read-and-critique is not slice-specific.
:::

---

# The Questions You Might Get

- **Critique** — "Read this diagram. What's wrong?" (yours or another team's)
- **Rationale** — "Why did you direct AI this way? What did you reject?"
- **Traceability** — "Walk this requirement to its test." / "Which requirement needs this class?"

Recall the strong answers: read aloud + fix; reason not authority; walk the trace and name a link you fixed.

::: notes
The question bank is the defense rubric (formalised later in Week 12). Point students at the strong-vs-weak patterns — the dry run is where they practise producing the strong version under pressure.
:::

---

# Use the Workshop Time

The first 50 minutes: close the gaps from your **Lab 5 checkpoint**.

- Re-run your **trace audit** — any broken links left?
- Re-read every diagram in your slice — practise critiquing it **aloud**.
- Rehearse your **rationale** for each real decision.

::: notes
Targeted prep, building on the checkpoint gaps. The aloud practice matters — defending is a spoken skill, and students who only think their answers stumble when asked live. Encourage peer mock-questioning during the workshop.
:::

---

# How to Use the Feedback

When you're called, you'll get **specific, actionable** feedback:

- what landed, what didn't;
- which defense skill you're weakest on (Critique, Rationale, or Traceability);
- the exact gap to close before Week 14.

Write it down. This is your **Week 14 checklist**.

::: notes
The deliverable is the feedback itself. Make sure students record it — the dry run's value is the concrete gap list per student. Frame weaknesses as fixable with two weeks to go.
:::

---

# Watch Others Defend

You learn as much watching as being called:

- Spot the **strong moves** (reading a multiplicity aloud, naming a fixed link).
- Spot the **red flags** ("the AI said it was good") — and make sure they're not yours.

::: notes
With random selection, not everyone is called equally — so framing observation as active learning keeps the room engaged and spreads the lesson. The red flags are easiest to internalise by hearing someone else trip on them.
:::

---

# Treat It As Real

- No slides, no notes, no AI — just you and your slice.
- A stumble here is **free**; a stumble in Week 14 costs points.

The dry run is the cheapest place to fail.

::: notes
The motivating frame. The dry run is a no-cost failure opportunity — students who take it seriously arrive at Week 14 having already made (and fixed) their mistakes. Discourage treating it as optional.
:::

---

# Why This Matters

Week 14 is graded and cold. Today is the same, free. Every gap you close now is points you keep then.

Next: **Week 13** workshop / Q&A to finish closing gaps; **Week 14** is the real defense (with Lab 7).

Closer. Connect directly to Week 14: the dry run is the last full rehearsal, Week 13 is buffer time to act on the feedback, Week 14 is the graded finale. The students who use today well defend cleanly.
