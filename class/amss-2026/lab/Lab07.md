# Lab 7: Final Presentations

100 minutes — **the graded finale.** Held together with the Week 14 lecture slot.

- **Team presentations**, back to back — each team presents its overview + each student their slice.
- **Per-student cold defense** — the Critique / Rationale / Traceability oral defense, graded.
- **Reflection** — what AI got right and wrong this semester.

This is where the **bulk of the project grade** is decided.

::: notes
The endgame. Everything since Week 1 led here: the team shows the system, each student defends their slice cold, and the Critique, Rationale, Traceability oral defense (3 of 8 project points) is graded live. The reflection segment closes the course's AI-mediated thesis.
:::

---

# The Format

Before we start: turn to your team and name the one defect you most want the examiner to see.

For each team, in turn:

1. **Team overview** (~5 min) — the system-level model; how the slices fit.
2. **Per-student slice** (~5 min each) — your requirement, your direction, what you caught, your trace.
3. **Cold defense** — the examiner asks Critique / Rationale / Traceability questions about your slice (and any diagram).

::: notes
The structure mirrors the per-team and per-student deliverables in the project description. The team owns the overview; each student owns their slice presentation and defense. Keep slice presentations to the 5-min shape from Week 12.
:::

---

# What's Graded (the Project Rubric)

Your project grade was defined in the project description — the defense is the largest live piece:

- **Oral defense (3 pts)** — Critique, Rationale, Traceability demonstrated cold. *The integrity check.*
- **Narrative + defect log (2 pts)**, **documentation (1 pt)**, **patterns (1 pt)** — from your repo.
- **Checkpoint (1 pt)** — already taken in Lab 5.

::: notes
Point students to `proiect/README.md` — Lab 7 does not introduce a new rubric, it applies the project rubric. Today decides the 3-point oral defense plus confirms the artifact points from the repo. Emphasise: the defense is graded cold, unaided.
:::

---

# Present the Trail (Week 12)

Lead with what you **did**, not just the final diagram:

- the requirement you own;
- how you **directed** AI, and what you **rejected**;
- a **defect you caught** and fixed;
- your **trace**, requirement to test.

The polished diagram is the result; the trail is the grade.

::: notes
The Week 12 presentation principle, restated for the real thing. Students who show only clean artifacts hide the graded work. The rejected output and caught defect are the evidence — lead with them.
:::

---

# The Cold Defense

Unaided, no AI. The examiner may:

- show you **any** diagram (Critique) — read it, critique it;
- ask **why** you directed AI a certain way (Rationale);
- ask you to **walk a trace** (Traceability).

You rehearsed this in the Lab 5 checkpoint and the Lab 6 dry run. Same skill, now graded.

::: notes
The defense is the Week 14/Lab-7 graded version of the checkpoint (Lab 5) and dry run (Lab 6). Nothing new — the students who used those rehearsals defend cleanly. Reassure: it is the same rubric they have practised twice.
:::

---

# The Reflection Segment

Each team closes with a short reflection:

- Where did AI **help** most across the semester?
- Where did it **fail** — and how did your critique catch it?
- What would you direct differently next time?

::: notes
The spec's Week 14 reflection segment, run here. It is not graded, but it consolidates the course thesis — students articulate, in their own words, the architect-and-critic value they added. Keep it short and candid.
:::

---

# Logistics

- Bring nothing but your repo — no AI, no new slides needed beyond your slice presentation.
- Teams present back to back; **be on time** — the schedule is tight.
- Every student must be present for their own defense.

::: notes
Practical notes. With many teams in 100 minutes, the schedule is unforgiving — the instructor will post an order. Absence means no defense, which means losing the 3-point integrity check. Make this clear in advance.
:::

This is the moment the whole course was built for: you present an **AI-mediated design** and defend it as its **architect and critic** — cold, unaided, in your own words.

That's the skill AI can't do for you, and the one that matters.
